The journey

I recently bought my first 3D printer and have been learning the ropes by printing parts for my other projects — Pi Cam boxes, cases for Pi 4 / 5 / 3, flex cam mounts, tilt‑pan mounts, and components for my SO101 arm. After dozens of downloaded models, I felt ready to try my own design.

Design & CAD

My first self‑designed part was a simple stencil — just a silhouette extruded to 3D. Easy. But the real challenge came next: designing replacement belt holders for an old Makeblock laser cutter (circa 2015).

I spent a few hours learning FreeCAD (Fusion 360’s free tier was too limited for my needs). As an open‑source fan, I can honestly say — FreeCAD is fantastic for this kind of mechanical part design.

The holes came out a bit tight — I used 8 mm for M4 nuts, but 8.2 mm would have made assembly smoother. Lesson learned for v2!

Print & result

The first print of the belt holders was a success! The parts fit the laser cutter’s mechanics, and with a little persuasion (and a few gentle taps) the M4 nuts went in. It’s incredibly satisfying to see a part you designed on screen become a physical object that actually works.

First original design → successfully printed & test‑fitted.
The next big challenge

Now that the mechanical parts are ready, the real work begins: getting the laser cutter itself back to life. The software hasn’t been updated since 2017, so I’ll need to dig into the codebase and see what can be revived.

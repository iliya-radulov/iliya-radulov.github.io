NOTICE No. BW-01 · BUREAU OF BORROWED WORDS · NOMENCLATURE REVIEW

Notice of Redesignation: Three Words Reviewed, None Reinstated

Filed during Data Ethics Week, on the case of three compound terms claiming descent from older, harder-won disciplines. The Bureau's finding, after review:

These aren't hybrid disciplines. They're branding decisions.

A hybrid discipline inherits the parent's actual cost — the part that makes the parent slow, embarrassing, and occasionally wrong in public. Science risks being disproven. Ethics risks reaching an answer nobody wanted. Art risks the maker's name on something that can fail as a statement, not just underperform as a product. A branding decision keeps the word and quietly drops the risk.

Cases reviewed

Term Claims descent from Bureau finding
Data Ethics Ethics Rejected — mostly compliance, pre-decided, rubber-stamped
Data Science Science Rejected, partial credit — real validation exists, but the label was adopted before the rigor was
AI Art Art Under evaluation — art has survived this exact fight before (photography, readymades); the harder objection is about whose training data, not whether skill was involved

Full deposition on all three, including checked sources: see the Data Ethics Case File.

Register extension, post-review

The Bureau notes the practice is not limited to disciplines. Individuals do it too, at scale, mostly on one particular professional network. A sample of self-applied titles currently in circulation, reviewed under the same standard — does the label carry the cost of the thing it's borrowed from, or only the credibility:

Self-applied title Bureau finding
Growth Hacker Rejected — growth, rarely hacking
Chief Vibes Officer Rejected — no chief, no office, some vibes
Serial Disruptor Rejected — disruption pending since 2019
Thought Leader Rejected — self-nomination is not a leadership mandate
Ecosystem Orchestrator Rejected — orchestra, ecosystem, and orchestrator all unconfirmed
Visionary Pending review — Bureau requires the vision in writing, dated, and falsifiable

The Bureau takes no position on whether any individual is, in fact, disrupting, orchestrating, or leading thoughts. Only on whether the title, by itself, is evidence of it. It is not. Neither, it turns out, is a compound noun with "data" in front of it.

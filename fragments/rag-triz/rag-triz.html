TRIZ + RAG: Asking Authors an Engineering Question

Small local Ollama-based RAG systems, each trained on a different author, all asked the same thing: explain a rigorous engineering methodology.

The idea

TRIZ (Teoriya Resheniya Izobretatelskikh Zadatch — "Theory of Inventive Problem Solving") is a genuinely systematic methodology for creative problem-solving: 40 documented inventive principles, developed by studying patterns across tens of thousands of patents. It's about as far as you can get from vibes-based creativity — structured, repeatable, engineering-grade.

The experiment: build a small local retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system per author, using Ollama, grounded in a subset of that author's actual writing — then ask each one the same plain engineering question about TRIZ, and see what comes back. Not full bibliographies (that's a lot of training time for a side project), just a representative slice per author, with more being trained in the coming days.

Honest status

First results weren't as impressive as hoped — a RAG system answering an engineering question is, most of the time, just... a RAG system answering an engineering question. But the potential is real, especially combined with the voice/vision work already underway elsewhere on this site: a spoken question, answered in a specific author's actual retrieved voice, is a much more natural and interesting interface than a text box. This is very much a tinkering project right now, not a finished one.

On language

Pelevin and Dostoevsky are both being run in their original Russian rather than English — round-tripping through translation loses too much of what actually makes each author's voice distinctive, and the whole point here is voice. English pages remain the entry point for readers, with the Russian-language RAG explorations linked alongside.

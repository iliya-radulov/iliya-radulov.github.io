Abstract

A single merged ternary description built from open binary CALPHAD assessments is only one of many that can be constructed from the published literature. For Nd–Fe–B, three Fe–Nd, seven Fe–B and four Nd–B assessments are freely available, giving 84 candidate ternary descriptions.

We screen all 84 for feasibility of Nd₂Fe₁₄B at the stoichiometric composition over 800–1600 K and report the resulting distribution of feasibility thresholds. Seventy-eight of the 84 combinations (92.9%) are mutually compatible under a strict (1 J mol⁻¹) merge-then-edge-check protocol. The six failures all involve the Witusiewicz 2019 Fe–B assessment — the only one that treats boron as purely interstitial in the disordered bcc, fcc and hcp phases — and arise because the merge silently alters the sublattice model of those phases; each deviates by only 6.2 J mol⁻¹ at a single test point. Re-admitting them leaves the range unchanged and shifts no median by more than 0.07 kJ.

At 800 K the ensemble threshold spans −9.26 to −8.33 kJ/mol (median −8.79, spread 0.94). The two available full ternary assessments (Hallemans 1995 and Chen 2019) both fall inside this range, with Hallemans coinciding with the median to within 0.01 kJ. The single-value threshold of −9.2 kJ/mol used in our prior Stage 3 work sits at the more-negative extreme of the ensemble. The spread grows from 0.94 kJ at 800 K to 2.35 kJ at 1600 K.

We argue that the ensemble is a minimal-assumption description of the current open thermodynamic data for Nd–Fe–B, and that single-value CALPHAD claims should be reported together with the ensemble spread around them.

01

Method

Each of the 84 combinations is merged with a parsed-database merge (not text concatenation), then verified against every binary edge. Combinations that fail the edge check are recorded but excluded from the feasibility screen.

Feasibility threshold

The threshold ΔGf*(T) is the Gibbs energy of formation, relative to the SER state, that a stoichiometric line compound at xFe=14/17, xNd=2/17, xB=1/17 must reach to compete with the equilibrium binary assemblage:

ΔGf*(T) = (1/1000) · [ Gcompeting(T) − Σi xi GiSER(T) ]   [kJ/mol, per mole of atoms]

A more negative threshold means the binary phases are already strongly stable and the compound must be strongly stabilised to appear; a less negative threshold means weaker stabilisation suffices. The screen uses pycalphad 0.11.2 and our tdb_merge.py utility.

Edge-check tolerance

The 1 J mol⁻¹ tolerance is deliberately tight relative to typical CALPHAD parameter precision and to numerical noise of the equilibrium solver, so that any detectable change in a binary edge is flagged. The six excluded combinations deviate by at most 6.2 J mol⁻¹; re-admitting them does not change the conclusions (§3.1).

Which edge drives the spread?

An additive model with the three edge choices as categorical factors attributes variance as follows (remainder is interaction: 4% at 800 K, 7% at 1000 K, ≤1% from 1300 K up):

800 K — Fe–Nd 51% · Fe–B 26% · Nd–B 20%

1000 K — Fe–Nd 30% · Fe–B 36% · Nd–B 26%

1300–1600 K — Fe–B dominates at 67 / 58 / 56 / 66%

The dominant edge therefore changes with temperature: Fe–Nd at 800 K, Fe–B at 1000 K and from 1300 K upward.

04

Failure mechanism

The six failures are all combinations of FeB-19Wit with NdB-19Che or NdB-16Zho-corrected. FeB-19Wit differs from the other Fe–B assessments in two ways; only the second causes failures.

not the cause

Extra Fe–B polymorph

FeB-19Wit models the mono-boride as MB_B27 (FeB-type, Pnma) and MB_B33 (CrB-type, Cmcm); the other six model only MB_B27. The extra phase is carried into every merged description but does not cause edge-check failures: all six combinations with NdB-95Hal-LB or NdB-98Che pass.

the cause

Silent change of the phase model

FeB-19Wit treats boron as purely interstitial in FCC_A1, BCC_A2, HCP_A3: (Fe)(B,VA). NdB-19Che and NdB-16Zho-corrected treat it as substitutional: (B,Nd)(VA). The merge unites constituents to (B,Fe,Nd)(B,VA) and adds four end-member parameters, without reporting a conflict. The Fe–B edge then deviates by 6.21 J mol⁻¹ at one of 30 test points (1600 K, xFe=0.95).

Decisive test. Restoring the original FeB-19Wit constituents on the three merged phases removes the deviation completely (0.0 J mol⁻¹ at all 30 points, with or without the four added parameters); deleting only the four added parameters leaves 2.5 J mol⁻¹. The unified constituent set is the cause; the added parameters are part of its effect. The change is silent because the merge reports it neither as a conflict nor as an error.

The same union produces a mixed model in 36 of the 78 survivors (substitutional Fe–B with interstitial Nd–B). These pass the edge check, so their induced energy change is below tolerance, but they are not validated beyond it. Neither feature is a bug in pycalphad or in the individual assessments; the failure arises from combining assessments that model boron on different sublattices.

05

Reproducibility

The TDB files are part of the open SGTE binary collection (Hallstedt 2025) and are also distributed by the ORCHID project. The SHA-256 digests of all 14 binary files are listed in Supplementary Table S1a (and the ternary files used for the overlay in S1b), so the 84 combinations are exactly reproducible; the four Nd–B digests agree with those published by ORCHID. The contribution of this work is the merge-then-edge-check protocol, the threshold kernel, and the per-combination results. 

# from stage_three/stage4_step1/ (full steps: README.md)
python run_batch_v2.py                          # 84 combinations, 8 workers
python diagnostics/extract_all_T.py             # thresholds of the 78 survivors, 8 temperatures
python diagnostics/sensitivity_relaxed.py       # re-admit the six failures
# → results_v2/batch_results.csv, results_v2/detail/<combo_id>.json, results_v2/summary.json

Runtime: 7.1 min summed per-combination, ≈1 min wall clock on 8 workers (5.1 s mean, 7.9 s max). A shared intermediate-filename race condition under parallel execution was fixed by naming each intermediate file by combo_id.

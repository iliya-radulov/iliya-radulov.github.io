Time Series Forecasting

Two forecasting studies completed during my data science training at WBS Coding School, October–November 2026.

Scope: Classical and modern forecasting methods applied to real-world time series — air quality monitoring and electricity market prices. Both studies compare baselines, ARIMA-family models and retraining strategies, and both find that capturing the right seasonal structure matters more than model complexity.

Projects

Forecasting Daily NO₂ in London

School project — 14 years of daily nitrogen dioxide measurements from North Kensington. Baselines, ARIMA, SARIMA and Prophet compared on a full hold-out year. Prophet wins because it captures the yearly heating cycle that dominates the signal.

Best MAE: 6.86 µg/m³ (Prophet) · Test year 2023
Retraining Matters More Than the Model

Own project — day-ahead electricity price forecasting for the German market using grid-operator forecasts of wind, solar and load. A gradient-boosting model retrained every 14 days cuts the error in half compared to a single fit, and beats a model trained once from July onward.

Best MAE: 15.98 €/MWh (retrained) vs 31.11 (naïve) · Oct 2025–Oct 2026

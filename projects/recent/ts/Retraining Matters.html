The market

A price that keeps changing its rules

After the 2021–22 gas crisis the German price settled at a higher level than before, while wind and solar push more hours below zero. In the test year the gap between the cheapest and the most expensive hour of a day grew from about 65 €/MWh in winter to over 200 €/MWh in late summer.

The main result

A model trained once wears out. Retrained, it stays ahead.

The same gradient-boosting model, with the same inputs. Trained once in October 2025, it was excellent in winter and worse than simply repeating yesterday's price from July. Retrained every 14 days on the last 180 days, it beat yesterday's price in every month, by 40–67%.

The inputs

What the model knows at noon the day before

Only information available before the day-ahead auction: the grid operators' published forecasts for tomorrow, today's and earlier prices, and the calendar. Wind and solar enter as shares of the forecast load, because 20 GW of wind means a surplus on a summer Sunday and only part of the demand on a winter weekday.

The comparison

How a model is trained decides which model wins

Mean absolute error over the full test year, lower is better. Trained once, the simple linear model beats gradient boosting. Retrained, gradient boosting wins clearly, because it can follow the curved merit order once it is kept up to date.

Robustness

Any window from 3 to 9 months works

The training window was chosen on the test year, so every length is reported. Between 90 and 270 days the error stays within 0.24 €/MWh, inside the uncertainty. With a full year of history it jumps by about 2 €/MWh: data older than nine to twelve months describes a market that no longer exists.

Findings

Four things this study shows

Yesterday's price is hard to beat

Repeating yesterday (31.1 €/MWh) beat Prophet with realistic inputs and the standard weekday-aware benchmark. The market's state lasts a few days; a week-old value is already outdated.

The drift is in the price response

The inputs stayed within familiar ranges. What changed was the price paid at a given residual load, which differed from the training period by up to 84 €/MWh in October 2026. That is what a single fit cannot follow.

Perfect weather adds almost nothing

With retraining, adding the actually observed weather on top of the grid operators' forecasts improved the error by at most 0.3 €/MWh.

Neither family wins alone

The winner is a supervised model with time-series memory in its inputs and time-series practice in its training.
